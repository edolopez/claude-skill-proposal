01 — The situation

Your visual system is set up for SMB. You're now selling to the Fortune 500.

Globex's identity was designed in 2019 for a 50-person bootstrapped product. It served you well to $24M ARR. But for the last six quarters you've been showing up in IBM and Deloitte boardrooms with materials that look like a Series A startup's — and your CRO says it's costing you in late-stage deals.

You don't need a full rebrand. You need a brand evolution that keeps the parts working (logo recognition, the Globex orange) and rebuilds the parts that aren't (typography, web, sales materials, customer-facing case studies).

"Three of our last five enterprise demos, the procurement person said something like 'are you guys actually big enough for this.' That's coming from how we look, not what we do."

03 — Our approach

Discovery, system, application — in that order.

Most agencies start with the website. We don't. Web is the visible output, but the failure mode you've described is the system underneath. Build the system first, apply it broadly, then maintain it.

01

Discovery · Month 1

Stakeholder interviews (8 internal, 5 customer), brand audit, competitor positioning, design archaeology. Output: a brand brief and three directional concepts.

Why this first: Skipping discovery is why most rebrands feel arbitrary. We need your CFO to see why every decision was made.

02

System · Months 2–3

Visual system design (typography, color, spacing, imagery, motion), component library, brand guidelines. Built directly in your Figma so handoff is zero-friction.

Why now: Once the system is locked, application is mechanical. Skip this and every page is its own redesign.

03

Application · Months 4–6

Marketing site rebuild (24 pages), sales deck templates (3 variants), case study template, email signature system, social toolkit, internal launch.

Why last: By month 4, your team has internalized the system. Application is a velocity exercise, not an interpretation exercise.

08 — Risks & how we handle them

What could go wrong

Risk

Stakeholder design opinions stall direction

5 execs with 5 design opinions is the most common rebrand killer.

Mitigation

CEO is the single design approver. Every other stakeholder voice routes through Mark + the CEO. Documented in week 1.

Risk

Sales team rejects new materials

If AEs don't use the new decks, it didn't ship.

Mitigation

Two AEs are on the design team review at Gate 2. Sales-deck workshop in month 5. Top-rep feedback shapes final variants.

Risk

Site migration data loss

CMS migration sometimes loses redirects, breaking SEO.

Mitigation

Pre-migration audit. 301 redirect map signed off by your SEO consultant. Staging URL in Search Console before cutover.
